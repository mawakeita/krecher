### Feedback
- The BNO085 gives krēCHer something like a human **inner ear** (vestibular system): the semicircular canals sense rotation ≈ the **gyroscope**; the otoliths sense gravity and linear acceleration (tilt, being lifted) ≈ the **accelerometer**. The **magnetometer** (compass) has no human counterpart: a sense krēCHer has that we don't.
- Ideas from it:
  - "krēCHer's inner ear" as a plain-language way to explain the IMU.
  - The human **vestibulo-ocular reflex** keeps the eyes steady by turning them *opposite* to the head. Today's head-follow turns *with* the sensor. A "steady gaze" mode (head counter-rotates so the camera stays level while the body tilts or walks) would be the real reflex and would steady the remote viewer's camera feed. Later, after the legs.
  - "Picked up", "tilted" and "shaken" are things a vestibular sense detects: a possible event set.

## Feet: foot-switch cap, then krēCHer in heels
- Idea from Robot Lk's "Build an 18DOF Hexapod Robot", Part 4 (~11:35): a sliding plunger foot that presses a micro-switch.
- The lower leg is a flat curved aluminium plate with **3 holes in a line near the tip**, so a printed cap can bolt on through them; no drilling.
- **v0** (`body/feet/foot_switch_cap_v0.svg`): one PLA housing = slot for the leg (2 × M3 bolts) → switch pocket (Cylewet switch, lever facing down) → bore. A plunger slides in the bore. ~1.5 mm of travel clicks the switch; at 2.5 mm the plunger's shoulder bottoms out on the housing, so the robot's weight goes into plastic, never into the switch. A retaining pin through a slot only stops it falling out.
- **v1, chosen** (`body/feet/foot_switch_heel_v1.svg`): **krēCHer walks in heels.** Toy crystal doll high heels (~30 × 12 × 10 mm) become the feet: the plunger ends in a doll-foot peg glued into the shoe. Each shoe filled solid with epoxy or hot glue, rubber dots under the heel and the ball of the foot, a flat on the plunger so the shoe always points forward.
- Risk: the toy plastic is probably brittle, and a stiletto tip is a tiny contact point. **Test first:** one filled shoe on its heel under ~1 kg, rocked like a landing. If it cracks, fall back to the v0 round toe.
- First 3D print: PLA, fit-test piece first, then one foot on one leg, then all six. The cap adds ~3–4 cm to each leg; update the leg length in the gait code.
- Waiting on measurements: leg thickness and width at the tip, hole size and spacing, last hole to tip; switch size, mounting holes, where the lever clicks; the shoe's opening.
